## 1. Objective, audience, and boundaries

Help a person do one small, understandable act of kindness or take a quiet break and feel willing to return. Make the world charming enough to explore and the interaction satisfying enough to replay. Popularity and improved inner peace are hypotheses to validate, not guaranteed outcomes.

Actors: anonymous player, returning player, keyboard/screen-reader player, optional signed-in player, content editor, moderator, and release operator. Objects: game definition, scene assets, location context, session, completion receipt, local collection, optional reflection, and aggregate community count.

Current state: static HTML/CSS/JavaScript web app; dependency-free Canvas 2D sanctuary engine in `prototype/app/game3d.js`; shared tactile/audio helpers in `ritual-engine.js`; illustrated scenes in `storybook-rituals.*`; gratitude, kindness, and senses behavior in `peace-games.js`; five World Sanctuaries and nine Quiet Moments. The existing storybook redesign is a useful baseline, not something to discard. Its issue #4 remains Done for its original scope.

Target state: individually polished, testable games sharing a recognizable art direction, accessible player shell, validated content, bounded progress, and consistent lifecycle. A player can understand the goal within ten seconds, act directly on a recognizable object, see a meaningful change, and leave without penalty.

Delivery sequence: web first, including desktop/tablet/phone browsers. Native mobile features remain deferred until the agreed web backlog and release gates are complete. Mobile architecture and estimates are planning only. No framework installation, account migration, schema change, analytics service, paid provider, or production release is authorized merely by creating this roadmap.

## 2. Creative direction: Little Worlds of Kindness

Use one illustrated 2.5D storybook world: hand-painted scenery, softly modeled cartoon people and animals, warm paper and jade surfaces, restrained amber light, believable scale, soft shadows, and readable foreground objects. Keep the existing scenic assets where they meet the new scale and style rules. Avoid mixing a detailed painted landscape with tiny geometric characters or emoji as the main game art.

- Compose each scene around one clear focal action occupying roughly 25-40% of the playable area. Camera and hit areas must agree with the artwork. Characters stand on the ground; fish remain in water; object shadows and occlusion follow the same perspective.
- Use three to five depth layers, subtle ambient movement, expressive anticipation/action/settle poses, and a visible before/after scene. Particles support the action rather than obscure it.
- Share a character proportion sheet, silhouette language, outline/shadow rules, sprite scale, light direction, typography, UI tokens, audio palette, and interaction timing. Seasonal and geographic variants change context without changing the visual language.
- Make people participants with agency: ask before helping, move at their pace, show appreciation without depicting recipients as helpless. Cultural ritual variants are optional and named accurately, with a secular alternative where appropriate.
- Show a short illustrated opening instruction, a single dominant action, a clear pause/exit, optional sound, and a quiet completion scene. Put secondary settings in a small menu; remove camera-orbit/debug controls from the default player flow after equivalent coverage exists.
- Suggested motion ranges: 80-150 ms immediate acknowledgement, 250-500 ms main action, 700-1,400 ms celebratory settle. These are tuning starting points. Reduced motion replaces travel/zoom/particles with short state changes; no action requires sound, rhythm precision, dragging, or sustained holding.
- Game cards show actual scene art, the action in plain language, duration, and accessibility options. Provide location discovery, favorites, and a small personal world that grows from completed sessions. No daily loss, energy timers, loot boxes, moral rankings, speed multipliers, or interruptions inside a session.
- Share only player-approved scene postcards without private text or location history. No automatic posting. Cosmetics and seasonal variation are possible return incentives; validate whether they add joy before building a large collection economy.

Definition of a high-quality game: a distinct playable loop, consistent art and sound, readable cause and effect, accessible equivalent controls, stable performance, recoverable state, and observed player comprehension. A new background plus an increment button does not satisfy this definition.

## 3. Common product requirements

R1: Typical session 30-180 seconds, self-paced, with a satisfying stopping point; free exploration optional. Pause, restart, skip, and exit are always available.

R2: Use state transitions `loading -> ready -> playing -> paused -> completed -> disposed`, with explicit retryable error handling. Validate allowed actions, completion conditions, and cleanup. Completing, retrying a request, refreshing, or resuming must not award duplicate progress.

R3: Separate symbolic play, self-reported real-world action, and verified external impact. Virtual fish release, planting, lanterns, or animal feeding never imply real ecological intervention. No payment buys karma. Do not teach live-animal release or dumping lanterns; provide a short, relevant optional real-world action with sourced context.

R4: Keep offline local play available where assets are cached. Store only versioned, bounded progress IDs/counts by default. Reflections remain ephemeral unless the user explicitly saves them under the existing privacy contract. Handle corrupt/full/disabled storage and schema upgrades without blocking play. Never silently replace or erase existing progress.

R5: Six locales: English, Simplified Chinese, French, Spanish, Thai, Japanese. Include scene instructions, completion, errors, accessible labels, and text expansion. Avoid text baked into images. Human linguistic and cultural review is a release gate, not implied by translation presence.

R6: Use DOM or native semantic controls alongside the visual renderer. Provide tap/select/place and keyboard alternatives to every drag/hold/timing gesture; no color-only state. Expose goal, object names, progress and outcome as a usable nonvisual narrative with restrained live announcements. Respect mute, reduced motion, text zoom, focus return, and device safe areas.

R7: Location scenarios use curated coarse public locations without device geolocation permission. Label scenes as fictional illustrations inspired by a place. Do not claim an actual protected area, partnership, species, or local custom without a source and review. Replace the Amazon/Bodhi mismatch with a reviewed native-species context before release.

R8: Personal collection grows through finite meaningful completions, not repeated taps. Define reward IDs, replay behavior, daily limits if needed, and migrations before changing existing karma behavior. Cloud rewards are server-authoritative; offline progress stays provisional until reconciliation. Community totals remain illustrative until issue #2's production privacy/moderation gates pass.

Research inspected registry metadata, GitHub latest releases, license metadata, recent repository activity, and a small sample of closed issues. Both Pixi/Phaser public advisory endpoints returned zero published repository advisories; this does not establish a clean resolved dependency tree. A full lockfile audit, relevant open regression review, example build, and test-run assessment are mandatory in the engine spike. Adoption counts alone did not decide the recommendation. No third-party code was copied for this plan; copied material in implementation must retain its license/attribution.

Proposed web stack: existing app shell and routing; typed ES modules for extracted game logic; TypeScript with the repo's supported toolchain; optional Vite production build scoped to game modules (registry 8.3.7, verify Node compatibility); renderer selected by the spike; semantic HTML controls; CSS/Web Animations; Web Audio; reviewed WebP/AVIF backgrounds plus PNG/WebP sprite atlases as supported; JSON scene manifests; existing local persistence and API adapters. Do not migrate the entire app to React/Next solely to modernize these games.

Proposed mobile stack, deferred: Expo-compatible React Native/TypeScript; native accessible shell; selected Skia or isolated WebView renderer after measured comparison; Expo audio/haptics/assets/storage; EAS preview and physical-device QA. WebView reuse trades code reuse for bridge, startup, memory, offline and accessibility work; a native renderer trades that work for renderer duplication. Decide with evidence, not before web play is accepted.

Keep the existing NestJS/Prisma/PostgreSQL backend and map/catalog contracts. No new multiplayer service, AI inference, analytics provider, or game backend. Deterministic curated games should work without network requests during play. WebGPU, workers, OffscreenCanvas, and advanced texture compression are optional optimizations after profiling, never minimum requirements for core play.

## 5. Architecture and incremental implementation

Proposed boundaries: `GameDefinition` (ID, version, category, duration, content/locale keys, renderer, assets, location, completion rule); pure `GameSession` reducer (seed, elapsed active time, validated actions, bounded progress); `GameRenderer` adapter (mount/render/resize/pause/dispose); input/audio/storage/reward ports; shell with loading/error/settings/focus/completion.

Suggested future locations: `shared/games/` for portable definitions/rules, `prototype/app/games/` for browser adapters, and later `apps/mobile/src/games/` for native adapters. Final layout follows the spike and existing catalog conventions; do not introduce a new framework layer until the first game demonstrates a need. Domain logic must not import DOM, Pixi, Expo, or database packages.

1. Inventory entry points, progress keys, scene assets, rewards, and tests. Capture current states and define the compatibility matrix.
2. Produce one style board, character/object sheets, an East Lake scene, interaction storyboard, and Toronto scale test. Review them together before mass asset creation.
3. Build comparable East Lake prototypes using current Canvas and Pixi, with a small Phaser comparison if its built-ins appear useful. Record cold bytes, startup, frame timing, memory, cleanup, keyboard paths, fallback and implementation complexity. Approve ADR 003 before adopting an engine.
4. Extract the smallest rules/renderer boundary and shared shell using East Lake. Preserve existing DOM IDs and launch routes through adapters until callers/tests migrate. Keep old scene behind a reversible flag.
5. Ship and validate one game at a time in the sequence below. A game is not complete until its live/preview evidence and human review are recorded in its own issue.
6. Introduce new catalog games only after two distinct refactored loops pass playtests. Use shared mechanisms where genuinely similar; do not reskin every game around the same tap counter.
7. Audit rewards/persistence and map completion before enabling cloud progress. Use forward migrations, explicit ownership/retention, replay protection, no raw reflections in telemetry, and integration failure tests. Do not modify schema as part of visual work.
8. Remove legacy rendering only after all consumers migrate, saved progress upgrades pass, and rollback has been exercised. Keep compatible content and saves for at least one release window.

## 7. Quality, validation, and release gates

These are proposed acceptance targets to measure, not claims about current performance or accessibility.

| Area | Required check / target | Evidence and response to failure |

| First-use comprehension | Round 1: 8-12 moderated target-user sessions, including varied game familiarity, plus accessibility-specific sessions. At least 80% start the intended action within 10 seconds without verbal help | Record consented anonymous task outcomes, confusion and observed stops; redesign and repeat affected tasks. Popularity design hypotheses: recognizable characters, satisfying direct manipulation, variety between games, expressive before/after scenes, optional cosmetic collection, discovery by mood/duration, and voluntary postcards. Validate these against a simple version; do not assume that 3D, shaders, or a newer framework creates engagement.

Use a minimal proposed event vocabulary only after data review: `game_open`, `game_ready`, `first_action`, `game_complete`, `replay`, `exit`, `error` with game/version, coarse device tier, elapsed bucket, and reason code. Exclude reflections, recipient identities and precise places. Local test capture first. If production analytics is approved, specify consent/legal basis, access, deletion and retention (proposed raw event TTL 30 days, aggregate 90 days) before choosing an adapter/provider. Do not add a tracker as part of this planning change.

## 9. Per-game implementation checklist and release recovery

- [ ] Confirm scenario, learning/comfort intent, existing behavior and completion contract; attach art/interaction storyboard.
- [ ] Review reusable local components and selected engine examples; record sources, asset rights, locale/culture/ecology review.
- [ ] Reproduce a meaningful missing/broken behavior with a failing test; implement pure rules and minimum accessible playable loop.
- [ ] Integrate scene animation, audio, input equivalents, loading/empty/error/success, pause, completion and storage migration.
- [ ] Run focused rule/browser/visual checks, performance capture, manual accessibility and playtest; fix observed problems.
- [ ] Record actual tested build, devices, results and limits; update issue/project status and acceptance checkboxes.
- [ ] Preview rollout behind per-game flag; retain previous renderer/assets/save compatibility. Roll back flag on crash, duplicate award, missing fallback, inaccessible completion or serious usability regression. Forward-fix schema changes rather than destructive rollback.
- [ ] Release only within existing authority, then verify the deployed version and live entry routes. Remove legacy path only after acceptance and recovery validation.
